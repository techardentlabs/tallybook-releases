Backup, restore and data safety in TallyBook

Techardent Labs · 3 November 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer: Your TallyBook data stays on your own computer and is never uploaded. Press Alt+Y → Backup to save a copy as one file, keep copies on a pen drive and in a cloud folder, and use Alt+Y → Restore to bring one back. Add users and passwords from Alt+K if others use the computer.

Accounting data is the one thing on a business computer you cannot download again. TallyBook keeps it local and makes backups a two-key job. This page explains where the data is, how to protect it, and the honest limits of the current version.

Where your data lives

TallyBook stores the company in a database file inside its application data folder on your computer. Nothing is sent to us or to any cloud service. The internet is used only to check for app updates, and updating keeps your data.

Backup

  1. From the Gateway press Alt+Y to open Data.
  2. Press B (Backup). A save window opens with a name like Sharma Traders 2026-11-03.tbbackup.json.
  3. Choose a pen drive, external disk or a synced cloud folder and save. TallyBook confirms "Backup saved".

Imports also offer Backup Company Data before Import, which is on by default.

Automatic scheduled backup is not available yet, so make it part of closing the day.

Restore

  1. Alt+Y → R (Restore) and pick the backup file.
  2. TallyBook warns: "Restoring replaces ALL data in this company with the backup." Choose Yes only if that is what you want.
  3. It reports how many records were restored. Check the Trial Balance and Day Book.

Tip: before restoring an old backup to look something up, take a fresh backup of today's data first.

Edit log: who changed what

Alt+Y → E opens the Edit Log, a list of changes to masters and vouchers, latest first, with date and time, action, type, particulars and user. Ctrl+F filters it.

Users and passwords

  1. Alt+K → Company Security. Set Use Security Control to Yes and enter the Name of Administrator and password.
  2. Users and Passwords: Alt+C creates a user, and you pick a role for them, such as Owner or Data Entry. User Roles lists the roles.
  3. Set rules such as Minimum password length under Password Policy, and fine-tune what each role may do in Security Control.
  4. Anyone can use Change User and Change Password from the same menu.

A user without access to a report or action sees "You do not have access" instead.

The TallyVault password, honestly

In Company Security you can also set a TallyVault password. TallyBook then asks for it before the company opens. In the current version this works as an opening password: the data file and backup files are not encrypted yet, and backups are readable files. For real protection against a lost or stolen laptop, turn on your operating system's disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on Mac, disk encryption on Linux) and keep backup drives somewhere safe. Keep your passwords written down securely; they never leave your computer, so we cannot reset them for you.

TallyBook is single-user: one person works in a company on one computer at a time. Do not put the live data folder in a shared or syncing drive that two computers open together. Share backup files instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is my TallyBook data uploaded anywhere?

No. It stays on your computer. The app goes online only to check for updates.

Where should I keep backups?

In at least two places besides the computer, for example a pen drive and a cloud folder, with one copy away from the office.

Does restoring merge data?

No. Restore replaces all data in the company with the backup. Take a fresh backup first if you may need today's entries.

Does the TallyVault password encrypt my data?

Not yet. In the current version it asks for a password before the company opens. Use your operating system's disk encryption for protection at rest.

Can I set up automatic backups?

Not in the current version. Take a backup with Alt+Y → B at the end of each day.
