Free accounting software for small businesses in India: what to look for

Techardent Labs · 3 November 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer: Look for proper GST invoicing and return reports, data kept on your own computer with easy backups, fast keyboard entry, a way to bring in your existing data, and clearly stated limits. TallyBook is free, works offline in the Tally style and suits one person keeping the books; it is not yet multi-user and does not file returns online.

"Free" covers very different things: a trial that locks you out after 30 days, a free tier with an invoice limit, an app that is free because it sells your data, or software that is simply free to use. For a small business, the right choice depends less on the price tag and more on whether your books will be correct, safe and yours. Here is a checklist you can use for any product, including ours.

1. GST that matches how you actually file

You need GST invoices with correct CGST, SGST or IGST, HSN/SAC codes, and reports that line up with GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. Ask: does it reconcile purchases with GSTR-2B? Can it export GSTR-1 in the portal's format? Does it handle credit notes, unregistered customers and reverse charge on purchases?

TallyBook today: GST invoices (accounting and item-wise), GSTR-1 with JSON export, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B reconciliation, HSN summary, and e-invoice and e-way bill JSON. You upload and file on the portal yourself. See Preparing GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.

2. Where your data lives

Online software keeps your books on the vendor's servers; desktop software keeps them on your machine. Neither is wrong, but know which you are choosing, and check that you can get all your data out in a standard format if you leave.

TallyBook today: data stays on your computer; nothing is uploaded. Export to Tally-style XML and CSV is built in.

3. Speed of entry

If you post hundreds of vouchers a month, the keyboard matters more than the look. Test it: can you enter a payment, a sales invoice and a journal without touching the mouse?

TallyBook today: keyboard-first, with Tally-style keys. See the shortcut cheat sheet.

4. Backups you will actually take

If backing up is fiddly, it will not happen. Look for a one-step backup to a file you can copy anywhere, a restore you have tested, and an audit trail of changes.

TallyBook today: Alt+Y → Backup and Restore, and an Edit Log. No automatic scheduled backup yet.

5. Getting your old data in

Re-typing opening balances and masters is where switching fails. Check for import from your current software and from Excel, with a preview before anything is written.

TallyBook today: imports Tally XML (masters and vouchers) and Excel/CSV, with a preview and no duplicates on re-import.

6. Honest limits

Every product has gaps. A good vendor writes them down. Ask about multi-user use, platform support, statutory return files and support.

Frequently asked questions

Is TallyBook really free?

Yes. There is no licence fee, no yearly renewal and no feature locked behind payment.

Does free accounting software handle GST properly?

Some do and some only make invoices. Check for GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B support. TallyBook prepares all three; you file on the GST portal.

Can my accountant work with TallyBook data?

You can share backups, CSV exports of any report, and Tally-style XML exports. Ask your CA which they prefer.

Is offline software safe?

Your data is as safe as your computer and your backups. Take regular backups, keep one copy off-site and turn on disk encryption.
