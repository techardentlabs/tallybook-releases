Users, roles and passwords in TallyBook: securing your company data

Techardent Labs · 3 November 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer: Press Alt+K to open the Company menu. Turn on Security Control in Company Security with an administrator name and password, then add people in Users and Passwords and give each a role such as Owner or Data Entry. Password Policy sets strength and expiry, Security Control shows who can do what, and Data (Alt+Y) › Edit Log records changes.

When a staff member, a part-time accountant or a family member helps with the books, you may not want everyone to see the Balance Sheet, alter old vouchers or change company settings. TallyBook includes TallyPrime-style security: named users, roles that control what each user can do, a password policy and an edit log. This guide walks through each screen and is clear about what the current preview does and does not enforce.

TallyBook is a single-user app: one person works on a company at a time, on one computer. Users and roles decide who is at the keyboard and what they may do, not how many people can work at once.

The Company menu (Alt+K)

Press Alt+K, or click K: Company in the top bar. The menu has three sections:

Step-by-step setup

  1. Press Alt+K › Company Security. Set Use Security Control to Yes, enter the Name of Administrator, a Password and Repeat Password, and press Ctrl+A.
  2. Open Password Policy if you want rules: set Activate password policy to Yes, then minimum length, optional counts of letters, numbers and special characters, expiry days, and whether old passwords may be reused.
  3. Open Users and Passwords. Press Alt+C to create a user: choose the User Role, type the Username and Password, and accept.
  4. Review Security Control to confirm what each role can reach.
  5. Close and reopen the company. TallyBook now asks for a username and password before showing the Gateway.

To change a user later, highlight them in Users and Passwords and press Enter; leave the password blank to keep the current one. F12 on the user form shows two more switches: Status (Active) and Password Policy applicable. Alt+D removes a user.

Roles and what they allow

Every user has a role. TallyBook ships with the role names TallyPrime users know. The ones that matter for a desktop-only setup are Owner, Data Entry and the read-only Auditor role (named Tally.NET Auditor for compatibility):

In User Roles, press Enter on a role to adjust it: base it on another role's facilities, then list facilities to disallow or allow, with the access level for each. When a user tries something their role does not permit, TallyBook shows “You do not have access to this action” or “You do not have access to this report”.

What is enforced in this preview: creating, altering and deleting vouchers and accounting masters, and opening the main reports (Balance Sheet, P&L, Trial Balance, Group Summary, Day Book, Stock Summary, Cash Flow and Funds Flow). Some fields are stored for TallyPrime compatibility but have no effect yet, because TallyBook has no browser, remote or TDL features: Allow Browser Access, Allow Remote Access, Allow Local TDL Files, and the Tally.NET options. The back-dated voucher limits in a role are saved but not yet checked during entry.

Passwords are never stored as plain text. TallyBook keeps only a salted, slow hash (PBKDF2-SHA256), so even someone who copies the data file cannot read them.

TallyVault password

In Company Security, the TallyVault section asks for a password and a confirmation. Once set, TallyBook asks for it every time the company is opened, before the user login. Be aware of the current limit: the data file itself is not yet encrypted. Treat the TallyVault password as an extra lock on the app, and protect the computer with its own login and disk encryption (BitLocker, FileVault or LUKS).

Edit log

Press Alt+Y for the Data menu and choose Edit Log (E). It lists, latest first, every create, alter, delete and cancel of vouchers, ledgers and groups, with the date and time, the type, the voucher number or master name, and the user who did it. Press Ctrl+F to filter, for example by a username. The log shows that a change happened and who made it; it does not keep the old and new values side by side.

Keep regular backups whatever your security settings: see backup, restore and data safety. For the full list of keys, read TallyBook keyboard shortcuts, and for how TallyBook compares on multi-user work, TallyPrime vs TallyBook.

Frequently asked questions

Can two people work in TallyBook at the same time?

Not on the same company over a network. TallyBook is a single-user desktop app. Users and roles let several people take turns on the same computer, each with their own login and rights.

Does the TallyVault password encrypt my data file?

Not yet. In this preview the TallyVault password is asked for whenever the company is opened, but the data file on disk is not encrypted. Protect the computer itself with an operating-system login and disk encryption.

What happens if I forget the administrator password?

There is no recovery in the app. Keep the administrator password somewhere safe, and keep a recent backup made from Data (Alt+Y) › Backup.

What does the Data Entry role allow?

By default, creating and altering vouchers and viewing some reports such as the Day Book. It cannot create ledgers or open the Balance Sheet, P&L or Trial Balance, and cannot delete vouchers.
